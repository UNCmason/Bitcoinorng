Questions

Short answers about the pictures, the coin, and the board.

What is an O'Ranger?

One picture of a person, from the head to the shoulders. Each picture in the drawn set has its own mix of head, glasses, cloth, and color. One hundred are drawn. The full set stays under 999.

What does this have to do with $ORNG?

Bitcoin is orange. $ORNG is the coin, and it trades on BONS. O'Rangers are the people who go with that story. The coin and the pictures are different things.

Are the pictures CRC-20?

No. $ORNG is a CRC-20 coin. A coin is an amount. A picture is one inscription, so the collection uses CRC-21. Look reads coins. It does not list these pictures.

What is CRC-21?

A short note on Bitcoin that says an inscription belongs to a collection. The picture itself is an ordinary ordinal. Price, the size of the set, and rarity are not in that note. Those rules are written into each picture when it is made.

Are they on Bitcoin yet?

No. The hundred pictures on this site are drawings. None have been inscribed. Mint stays closed until the collection is signed onto Bitcoin.

How will mint work?

You pick a head, glasses, and a cloth color, see the picture, and pay from your own wallet. Two people cannot take the same mix at the same time. The site does not hold the wallet and does not send the payment.

What will one cost?

Bitcoin is $9 at the live rate. $ORNG is 20% under that, so $7.20 of $ORNG. The page can show today's bitcoin reading in sats. That reading is not a charge. The $ORNG amount is not invented here.

Why is $ORNG two steps?

Paying with $ORNG and receiving the picture are two Bitcoin transactions. They do not settle together. Bitcoin does: the picture and the bitcoin move in the same transaction.

What is rare?

The weights are fixed before the first mint and written into each inscription. A plain head, no glasses, and a simple shirt are the common ones. A hood, a visor, an eye patch, and bitcoin wear are less common. Rarity is not stored on the $ORNG coin.

Can two pictures be the same?

No. The full mix is the identity. Head, glasses, and cloth color already make many mixes, and the cloth cut makes more than 999. The set stops under 999, so each minted picture stays unique.

Where do they trade?

On Bitcoin, as inscriptions. This site has a small board for a signed bitcoin price. Other places that list ordinals do not copy this board. A collection also has to be reviewed before it shows on seals.garden. $ORNG itself stays on the BONS swap.

What does the board do?

A wallet that holds an inscription can list it. Buyers see that inscription and pay the seller's price in bitcoin. The site checks that the picture and the bitcoin move in the same transaction. The resale price is whatever the seller sets. It is not the $9 mint price. If nothing is listed, the board says so.

Can the board list something that is not an O'Ranger?

Yes. Until O'Rangers exist on Bitcoin, a listing is an inscription the connected wallet holds. The page says you are buying the inscription shown. O'Rangers appear there once they are on Bitcoin.

Does the site hold the picture or the bitcoin?

No. You sign in UniSat or OKX. Signing a listing does not send the picture. A buy is broadcast only after the checks pass, and it is the transaction you signed. This site does not take a fee.

What am I signing when I list?

One picture and one price, paid to the address that holds the picture. The wallet should show that and nothing else. If it will not sign, the listing does not go up and nothing is sent.

What am I signing when I buy?

The price, the miner fee, and the spare bitcoin that lets the picture move to you. The price and the fee are shown before the wallet opens. You need two small spare outputs that do not already hold a picture. If you only have one, the page can split it back to you. That split is not the purchase, and it has to confirm before the buy.

Can I pay the board with $ORNG?

No. The board settles in bitcoin only. An $ORNG payment would be a separate Bitcoin move, and it is not what this board does.

Why can't I raise a listed price?

The lower signature would still be valid. Send the picture to yourself first. That spends the old output, and then you can list the new one at a higher price. A lower price can replace the listing.

How do I take a listing down?

The board gives you a cancel code once, when the listing goes up. Keep it. You can also take the listing down by spending that output, including sending the picture to yourself.

Is any of this financial advice?

No. $ORNG is a meme coin. CRC-20 and CRC-21 on Bitcoin are experimental. You can lose what you put in.
